## Two Candidate Directions

### Direction A: Base-Rate Stabilization

**Goal:** Establish a reliable Base adherence rate for BENCH2-001.

**Rationale:** The Base rate varies between 40% (P090) and 80% (P112). Without a stable baseline, all condition comparisons are uncertain. A 20+ run Base replication would narrow the CI and establish whether the true rate is ~50%, ~70%, or something else.

**Cost:** ~60 calls (20 runs × 3 calls)
**Value:** Fixes the most fundamental measurement problem — we don't know the baseline.

### Direction B: Cross-Task E3a Replication

**Goal:** Test whether the broader-objective effect (E3a = 10/10) generalizes to other error-bearing tasks.

**Rationale:** E3a's 10/10 is the strongest signal in DIAGNOSTIC-006. If it replicates on BENCH2-002/004/005, task framing becomes a much stronger candidate mechanism. If it doesn't, the E3a result may be task-specific.

**Cost:** ~120 calls (3 tasks × 10 runs × ~3 calls, minus existing BENCH2-002 data)
**Value:** Tests generalizability of the strongest finding.

### Direction C: Both (Sequential)

Run Direction A first (cheap, fixes baseline), then Direction B (tests generalizability).

**Cost:** ~180 calls total
**Value:** Most comprehensive, but longest.

## Recommendation

**Direction A first.** The Base-rate instability is a measurement problem that undermines all other comparisons. Without knowing the baseline, we can't interpret any condition effect. Direction A is also cheaper and faster.

After Direction A, Direction B becomes much more informative because we'll have a stable baseline to compare against.
