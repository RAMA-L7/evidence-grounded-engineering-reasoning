Pilot

The one inferential test, and what it did not do.

The framing pilot P191-R4 was randomised and pre-registered before the runs. It asked a narrow question: does a broader task framing change the outcome compared with a narrow one? Three tasks were each run under both framings, eight times per cell.

The pooled result leaned toward broader framing by one third of a unit, and the direction was consistent across all three task blocks. The exact one-sided test returned a probability of 0.16265286, which does not meet a conventional threshold. It is published as positive in direction and not significant, and the causal claim stays closed.

A negative result, kept

The larger benchmark measured nothing, and that is published.

Stage A ran a budget-frozen four-arm comparison, 128 scheduled runs across four arms, four tasks and eight repetitions. Every completed run finished with a final worst slack of exactly 0.0 nanoseconds, in all arms. The primary contrast was therefore identically zero on the pre-registered estimand, with an exact p of 1.000.

The correct reading is that the metric could not discriminate on that substrate, so the result is recorded as a metric-power failure rather than as evidence that the arms were equivalent. The gate that would have authorised Stage B failed, and Stage B did not run.
